# FAQ

**My empire was doing fine. Why did it suddenly start to decline?**

Every dynasty builds up Decay, and the older it gets, the faster it ages. Even the best dynasty declines eventually. Losing wars, losing cities, a poor ruler, and Estranged vassals all speed it up. To hold on, win more wars, or wait for a level 5 ruler to start a Restoration. See [Rise and Fall of Dynasties](cycle.md).

**Why are cities rebelling one after another?**

In Decline, the lower the Mandate, the more city loyalty is lost, and the cities with the lowest loyalty rebel first. Also, if parliament passes policies that are too far from a clan's stance ("Opposed policies"), or moves troops and money to the center ("Curbed privileges"), that clan's cities become unhappy. Open a city's loyalty breakdown to see the reasons. See [Parliament and Policies](parliament.md#local-discontent).

**Why did a vassal suddenly become independent?**

A vassal plots independence when it is unhappy with its overlord and thinks the overlord cannot hold it down. An overlord in Decline, a vassal with deep resentment, an overlord fighting elsewhere, or a Vassal Tribute set too high can all make a vassal rise. Check the vassal's opinion breakdown and its Integration trait. See [Vassals](vassals.md#independence).

**Why did a large kingdom grant a city it had just taken to someone else?**

When a kingdom has more cities than vanilla's governing limit, most newly taken cities are granted away as vassals instead of being kept. To make a kingdom grant less, pass the Direct Rule policy in parliament. See [Vassals](vassals.md#where-vassals-come-from).

**Why won't my armies attack?**

When the Army policy is not Noble Retinues, local armies mainly defend the border and attack only when their strength is at least that of the enemy city's garrison. Campaigns are mostly done by the Guard. Also, cities and kingdoms with unpaid troops do not recruit. To make an army attack, just give it an order, see [Commanding Armies](commands.md).

**Why are my armies shrinking?**

It may be unpaid troops. When the treasury or a city stockpile cannot pay, some soldiers are discharged each year, and the rest deal less damage. Look at the unpaid troops notice in the kingdom and city windows. See [Treasury and Pay](treasury.md).

**A war has gone on for years without ending. Why?**

Peace talks only start after 3 years of war. The side ahead refuses if its expectation is higher than the war score, and warlike kings insist on fighting until the other side submits. But the longer the war and the more people die, the lower the expectation falls. Total War has no peace talks. See [War and Peace Talks](war.md).

**Why can't I take a big city?**

In vanilla, as long as one defender is in the city, progress is stuck at 5%. Now the attacking captain goes after the defenders, and when the attackers outnumber the defenders 5 to 1 the city falls outright. If you don't have enough troops, you still need to clear out the defenders first.

**How did a republic become an empire?**

An ambitious consul of a large republic at its Prime may crown himself or herself emperor. If you don't want such events, turn off "Historic upheavals" in the settings. See [Governments and Rulers](government.md#republic-crowns-an-emperor).

**My attack order was gone after I loaded a save. Why?**

Attack orders are not saved. After loading, armies go back to their previous behavior. Garrison and camp orders are saved.

**Do old saves work?**

Yes. Saves made before installing the mod can be loaded as they are, and the data the mod needs is filled in automatically in game: the government is rolled once according to culture, large kingdoms count as Prime, and existing vassals start as Estranged.
